\section{Critical examination of the source research}

The source, \textit{Sparks of Weight-Space Programming} (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang \& Khattab, MIT CSAIL, 2026), reports that frontier LLMs can ``read and write'' neural-network weights like code, a capability it terms \textbf{Weight-Space Programming (WSP)}. I assessed it before designing my experiment, focusing on the distinction the assignment highlights: \emph{reasoning about weights directly} versus \emph{optimising edits through repeated model feedback}.

\textbf{Strongest evidence.} The \emph{reading} results are the most impressive and best controlled: identical architectures, no game named, no forward passes and audited scripts (Experiments 1--3). In particular, naming a training language and locating exact copying heads from unlabelled \emph{middle} layers of a 7M-parameter character LM (E3) is the hardest to dismiss as memorisation.

\textbf{Most important limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The work is explicitly \emph{not} a method: it is a capability claim made with one model (GPT-6 Astra), mostly single runs, and weak baselines the authors themselves flag.
\item The headline language-model result (Qwen3.5-0.8B, MATH-500 7.9\%$\rightarrow$48.1\%) comes from fixing a known loop-failure, and independent replications found the effective edit largely acted \emph{at the output} (final-norm scaling, down-weighting ``Wait'', up-weighting ``\texttt{}''), i.e.\ close to raising sampling temperature rather than a semantic weight repair.
\item The authors' own additional experiment (Gemma 3 270M, which does not loop) could \emph{not} be improved: weight editing \emph{removed an obstacle that hid an existing ability but did not add an ability the model lacked}.
\item Nothing shows WSP scales to large models; the writing results are on tiny, symbolic, well-known toy tasks.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reasoning vs.\ feedback.} Most of the flashy gains (E6 Go, E8 math) use an iterative loop of forward passes plus score feedback --- an agentic search. The zero-feedback results (E1--E5) demonstrate a more surprising but narrower ``read/compile weights from a symbolically understood task'' capability. A credible experiment must separate these two regimes and pit WSP against the cheap alternatives SE.LLMA would try first (prompting, decoding, logit/activation steering, LoRA fine-tuning).

\section{Hypothesis}

\textbf{Claim (testable):} a frontier LLM (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) editing an open model's weights directly --- no gradients, no training data --- can improve the model's Swedish behaviour \emph{better than} prompt / decoding / logit-steering baselines, without degrading English, and the mechanism is weight-localised rather than a re-weighting of Swedish-token output logits.

\textbf{Why it matters.} SE.LLMA builds Swedish models by fine-tuning open models. WSP is useful only if it beats what SE.LLMA already does cheaply, and only genuinely interesting if it changes behaviour at the \emph{weights} rather than merely biasing next-token logits (a known, trivial ``language steering'' trick).

\textbf{Supporting evidence would be:} driven gains on held-out Swedish metrics beyond prompting/decoding/logit-steering; no reproduction by a random edit or output-layer rescaling; changed weights in MLP/attention rather than the output head; flat English regression.

\textbf{Challenging evidence would be:} the improvement is matched by (a) a Swedish prompt, (b) a decoding/logit-bias change, (c) a magnitude-matched random edit, or (d) output-layer-only edits; or English degrades.

A clean \emph{negative} result (WSP $\approx$ logit steering) is itself valuable: it tells SE.LLMA not to invest in WSP for language gating.

\section{Experiment design}

\textbf{Subject model:} \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B} (the paper's model; public, cached). (A Swedish-native second model, GPT-SW3, was blocked by Hugging Face gating and is deferred.) \textbf{Editor:} \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro-0813} via the Model Studio token plan.

\textbf{Arms} (all evaluated on one held-out set never shown to the editor; see Table~\ref{tab:arms}).

\textbf{Signals.} (1) \emph{Grammar probes} (V2 word order + gender/definite agreement, 16 held-out items) scored by log-likelihood --- a \emph{weight-level} signal insensitive to decoding. (2) \emph{Open-ended response-language} (8 Swedish prompts) scored by an offline Sv/En language-ID --- a \emph{surface} signal. (3) \emph{English regression} (10 factual MCQs) for forgetting. The contrast between (1) and (2) is the key diagnostic: a gain appearing in (2) but not (1) indicates a decoding/logit-level effect, not a genuine weight edit.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} One GH200 GPU, Apptainer \texttt{qwen\_inference.sif}, seed 0, full editor transcript dumped to \texttt{results/}; dev/eval probes separated.
