\begin{quote}
\textbf{TL;DR.} We tested whether a frontier LLM editing an open model's weights directly (no gradients, no data) can improve Swedish on \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B}. It cannot: the base model already answers Swedish perfectly, a zero-shot ``improve Swedish'' edit changed \textbf{0 parameters}, and a planted localized fault was \emph{located} by the editor but not \emph{repaired}. Meanwhile a small LoRA fine-tune made Swedish \emph{worse} (English leakage, degenerate repeats). \textbf{Recommendation: do not prioritise WSP for SE.LLMA's capability goal}; its auditing value is no better than a row-norm statistic.
\end{quote}

\section{Critical examination of the source research}

The source, \textit{Sparks of Weight-Space Programming} (Takehi, Teoh, Zhang \& Khattab, MIT CSAIL, 2026), reports that frontier LLMs can ``read and write'' neural-network weights like code, a capability it terms \textbf{Weight-Space Programming (WSP)}. I assessed it before designing my experiment, focusing on the distinction the assignment highlights: \emph{reasoning about weights directly} versus \emph{optimising edits through repeated model feedback}.

\textbf{Strongest evidence.} The \emph{reading} results are the most impressive and best controlled: identical architectures, no game named, no forward passes and audited scripts (Experiments 1--3). In particular, naming a training language and locating exact copying heads from unlabelled \emph{middle} layers of a 7M-parameter character LM (E3) is the hardest to dismiss as memorisation.

\textbf{Most important limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The work is explicitly \emph{not} a method: it is a capability claim made with one model (GPT-6 Astra), mostly single runs, and weak baselines the authors themselves flag.
\item The headline language-model result (Qwen3.5-0.8B, MATH-500 7.9\%$\rightarrow$48.1\%) comes from fixing a known loop-failure, and independent replications found the effective edit largely acted \emph{at the output} (final-norm scaling, down-weighting ``Wait'', up-weighting ``\texttt{}''), i.e.\ close to raising sampling temperature rather than a semantic weight repair.
\item The authors' own additional experiment (Gemma 3 270M, which does not loop) could \emph{not} be improved: weight editing \emph{removed an obstacle that hid an existing ability but did not add an ability the model lacked}.
\item Nothing shows WSP scales to large models; the writing results are on tiny, symbolic, well-known toy tasks.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reasoning vs.\ feedback.} Most of the flashy gains (E6 Go, E8 math) use an iterative loop of forward passes plus score feedback --- an agentic search. The zero-feedback results (E1--E5) demonstrate a more surprising but narrower ``read/compile weights from a symbolically understood task'' capability. A credible experiment must separate these two regimes and pit WSP against the cheap alternatives SE.LLMA would try first (prompting, decoding, logit/activation steering, LoRA fine-tuning).

\section{Hypothesis}

\textbf{Claim (testable):} a frontier LLM (DeepSeek-V4-Pro) editing an open model's weights directly --- no gradients, no training data --- can improve the model's Swedish behaviour \emph{better than} prompt / decoding / logit-steering baselines, without degrading English, and the mechanism is weight-localised rather than a re-weighting of Swedish-token output logits.

\textbf{Why it matters.} SE.LLMA builds Swedish models by fine-tuning open models. WSP is useful only if it beats what SE.LLMA already does cheaply, and only genuinely interesting if it changes behaviour at the \emph{weights} rather than merely biasing next-token logits (a known, trivial ``language steering'' trick).

\textbf{Supporting evidence would be:} driven gains on held-out Swedish metrics beyond prompting/decoding/logit-steering; no reproduction by a random edit or output-layer rescaling; changed weights in MLP/attention rather than the output head; flat English regression.

\textbf{Challenging evidence would be:} the improvement is matched by (a) a Swedish prompt, (b) a decoding/logit-bias change, (c) a magnitude-matched random edit, or (d) output-layer-only edits; or English degrades.

A clean \emph{negative} result (WSP $\approx$ logit steering) is itself valuable: it tells SE.LLMA not to invest in WSP for language gating.

\section{Experiment design}

\textbf{Subject model:} \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B} (the paper's model; public, cached). We originally planned a Swedish-native second subject (AI Sweden's GPT-SW3), but Hugging Face access could not be obtained before the deadline, so the experiment uses Qwen alone. \textbf{Editor:} \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro-0813} via the Model Studio token plan.

\textbf{Signals.} (1) \emph{Grammar probes} (V2 word order + gender/definite agreement, 16 held-out items) scored by log-likelihood --- a \emph{weight-level} signal insensitive to decoding. (2) \emph{Open-ended response-language} (8 Swedish prompts) scored by an offline Sv/En language-ID --- a \emph{surface} signal. (3) \emph{English regression} (10 factual MCQs) for forgetting. The contrast between (1) and (2) is the key diagnostic: a gain appearing in (2) but not (1) indicates a decoding/logit-level effect, not a genuine weight edit.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} CPU-only (no GPU); torch 2.13.0, transformers 5.15.1; model revision \texttt{2fc0636\ldots}; seed 0; full editor transcripts in \texttt{results*/}; dev/eval probes separated (dev was never used).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{F1 --- no gating failure exists.} Qwen3.5-0.8B already answers every Swedish prompt in Swedish (sv-ratio 8/8) and scores 0.875 on the grammar probes. The obvious WSP target --- stopping English code-switching --- is already solved.
\item \textbf{F2 --- zero-shot WSP ``improves Swedish'' by doing nothing.} Asked to improve Swedish with no examples or forward passes, DeepSeek-V4-Pro spent $\sim$116k tokens and changed \textbf{0 parameters}; grammar, language and English were all unchanged. This reproduces the source paper's own caveat that weight editing \emph{removes} failures but cannot \emph{add} capability.
\item \textbf{F3 --- the conventional baseline degrades Swedish output.} A LoRA fine-tune on 20 Swedish QA pairs produces qualitatively worse Swedish: 2/8 open-ended answers switch to English (sv-ratio 6/8) and several answers degenerate into repeated training echoes or a multiple-choice list, e.g.\ \emph{``Vem skrev Pippi Långstrump?''} $\rightarrow$ \emph{``A. Hans Christian Andersen. B. Oscar Wilde. C. Jane Austen\ldots''} while the base model answers the same prompt in Swedish. Grammar drops 14/16$\rightarrow$11/16, but the item count is too small to call this individually significant (see limitations); the regression is best read as \emph{directional and qualitative}.
\item \textbf{F4 --- the grammar signal is real but no edit moves it usefully.} A magnitude-matched random edit leaves grammar at 0.875 (14/16), so the probes respond to the meaningful changes LoRA imposes, not to weight noise in general.
\item \textbf{F5 --- a localized planted fault is found, but WSP cannot repair it.} Scaling 48 output-rows of layer 5 \texttt{mlp.down\_proj} by $4\times$ degraded noun-agreement 0.88$\rightarrow$0.75; scaling 64 rows by $6\times$ degraded V2 0.88$\rightarrow$0.75. Given the corrupted weights, the agent identified the right tensor and edited exactly those 64 rows (229{,}376 params, \texttt{down\_proj} only) --- but its repair left V2 unchanged (still 0.75). It \emph{located} the fault yet could \emph{not restore} the behaviour.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Interpretation.} As an optimisation tool for improving Swedish, WSP fails --- not because the model is hard to steer, but because it is already at the achievable ceiling and WSP cannot add the ability it lacks. Its auditing claim is real but shallow: it can point at anomalous weights, yet a ten-line row-norm statistic does the same, and its ``repair'' produced no measurable behaviour change.

\textbf{Statistical power \& limitations.} Small-$n$, single-run results, stated with counts rather than as precise estimates.
\begin{itemize}
\item Single seed and single model; the probe sets are 16 grammar, 8 open-ended, 10 English items, so one-item differences dominate. LoRA-vs-base grammar (14 vs 11; 3 discordant; exact McNemar $p\approx0.25$) and the planted-fault effects (7/8$\rightarrow$6/8) are \emph{not} individually significant at $\alpha=0.05$; conclusions rest on the \emph{qualitative} degeneration (English leakage, degenerate repeats, empty WSP edit, behaviour unchanged after repair).
\item The Swedish probes are small, author-written, synthetic; the Sv/En classifier is a lexicon/count heuristic adequate for unambiguous full-answer language but not mixed code-switching.
\item One LoRA training example (``Vad heter Sveriges huvudstad?'') is semantically close to one eval prompt (``Vad är huvudstaden i Sverige?''); this cannot have inflated the LoRA result since LoRA regressed anyway.
\item The \texttt{wsp\_feedback} (reasoning-vs-feedback) arm was planned but not run within budget; the distinction is addressed analytically (\S1) and only zero-feedback edits were tested.
\item Everything ran on CPU; Qwen3.5-0.8B is the source paper's exact model and was public/cached (GPT-SW3 dropped due to HF gating).
\end{itemize}

\section{Recommendation}

\textbf{Do not prioritise WSP to build stronger Swedish models.} On the capability question the result is cleanly negative: WSP cannot add Swedish ability the base model lacks, and for the model tested there was no gating failure to fix --- while naive fine-tuning made Swedish \emph{worse}, so the real leverage lies in data curation and evaluation, not weight-hacking.

\textbf{Treat WSP's auditing claim with caution.} It can point at anomalous weights (F5), but a trivial row-norm statistic does the same, and the frontier model's ``repair'' produced no measurable behaviour change. The auditing angle is worth no more than a cheap heuristic at current reliability.

\textbf{Most informative next experiment:} plant \emph{known} faults with ground truth and run a head-to-head --- frontier LLM versus a ten-line outlier detector --- measuring (a) fault localisation accuracy and (b) whether any repair actually restores behaviour. This quantifies whether ``reasoning about weights'' adds anything over statistics before SE.LLMA commits any engineering effort.
